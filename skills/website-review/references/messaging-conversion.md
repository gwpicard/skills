## Messaging & Content Strategy

### Value Proposition

**5-Second Test**: Can a first-time visitor understand what the site offers, who it's for, and why they should care within 5 seconds of landing?

Check for:
- **Above-the-fold clarity**: Primary headline communicates the core value, not a vague tagline
- **Specificity over abstraction**: "Helps B2B SaaS reduce churn by 30%" beats "Innovative solutions for modern businesses"
- **Audience signal**: Visitor should immediately know if this is for them
- **Differentiation**: What makes this different from competitors? Is it stated or implied?

Common issues:
- Jargon-heavy headlines that sound impressive but say nothing
- Leading with the HOW (features, technology) instead of the WHAT/WHY (outcomes, benefits)
- Generic stock-photo hero sections with no real content
- "We" focused copy instead of "You" focused copy

### Headline Hierarchy

- **H1**: One per page. Clear, benefit-driven, keyword-aware
- **H2s**: Section headers that tell a story even when skimmed
- **Subheadings**: Support scanning — a visitor reading ONLY headings should understand the page

### Benefit vs Feature Framing

| Feature (weak) | Benefit (strong) |
|----------------|-----------------|
| AI-powered analytics | Know exactly which customers will churn before they do |
| 24/7 support | Get help the moment you need it, day or night |
| Custom integrations | Works with the tools you already use |

Features should appear as supporting evidence under benefits, not as primary messaging.

### Brand Voice Consistency

Check across all pages:
- Does the tone match the audience? (Professional for B2B, approachable for personal brand)
- Is vocabulary consistent? (Don't say "clients" on one page and "customers" on another)
- Does the personality come through? (Especially for personal brands — generic = forgettable)

### Content Completeness

Essential pages and their purpose:
- **Homepage**: Hook, orient, direct
- **About**: Build connection and credibility
- **Services/Product**: Detail the offering with clear next steps
- **Pricing** (if applicable): Reduce friction, anchor value
- **Contact**: Make it dead simple
- **Blog/Resources** (if applicable): Demonstrate expertise, SEO fuel

## CTAs & Conversion

### CTA Evaluation Criteria

**Clarity**: Does the CTA tell the user exactly what happens next?
- Good: "Start your free trial" / "Book a 15-min call" / "Download the guide"
- Bad: "Learn more" / "Get started" / "Submit" / "Click here"

**Visibility**: Can the user find the CTA without searching?
- Above the fold on key pages
- Repeated at natural decision points (after benefits, after social proof, at page end)
- Visually distinct (contrast, size, whitespace)

**Hierarchy**: Is there a clear primary vs secondary CTA?
- One primary action per page (the thing you most want them to do)
- Secondary CTAs for visitors not ready for the primary action
- Don't present 5 equal options — that's no options

**Friction**: What stands between the user and conversion?
- Number of form fields (every field reduces completion)
- Account creation requirements
- Unclear pricing or hidden costs
- Lack of "what happens next" clarity

### Conversion Funnel Assessment

Map the intended journey:
1. **Awareness**: How does a visitor arrive? (SEO, referral, direct)
2. **Interest**: What keeps them on the page? (Relevance, hook, skimmability)
3. **Consideration**: What builds confidence? (Social proof, details, comparison)
4. **Action**: What's the conversion event? (Sign up, book call, purchase)
5. **Post-action**: What happens after? (Confirmation, onboarding, follow-up)

Look for leaks at each stage.

### Pricing Page (if applicable)

- Are plans clearly differentiated?
- Is there an obvious recommended plan?
- Are prices visible (not hidden behind "Contact us" unless enterprise)?
- Does it anchor value before showing price?
- FAQ section addressing objections?

## Trust Signals

### Social Proof Hierarchy (strongest to weakest)

1. **Specific results with names**: "We helped Acme Corp increase revenue by 47% in 6 months" — Jane Doe, VP Sales
2. **Named testimonials with photos/titles**: Real people with real credentials
3. **Client logos**: Recognizable brands they've worked with
4. **Aggregate numbers**: "500+ companies trust us" / "10,000+ users"
5. **Generic testimonials**: "Great product!" — J.D.
6. **No social proof**: A significant gap

### What to check

- **Testimonials**: Are they specific (mention results, context) or generic (vague praise)?
- **Case studies**: Do they exist? Do they follow problem > solution > result?
- **Logos**: Are they recognizable to the target audience?
- **Numbers**: Are they credible and specific? "500+ clients" beats "many clients"
- **Media mentions**: Press logos, "as seen in", podcast appearances
- **Certifications/awards**: Relevant industry credentials

### Authority Indicators

For personal brands:
- Bio with specific credentials, not just buzzwords
- Published work, speaking engagements, podcast appearances
- Professional headshot (not a logo for a personal brand)
- Specific experience ("15 years in B2B SaaS" not "extensive experience")

For SaaS/B2B:
- Team page with real people
- Security/compliance badges (SOC2, GDPR, etc.)
- Integration partners
- Investor/backer names (if notable)

### Risk Reducers

- Free trial or freemium tier
- Money-back guarantee
- "No credit card required"
- Demo or preview access
- Clear cancellation policy
- Privacy policy and terms

### Trust Breakers

Missing proof lowers trust slowly; manipulative design destroys it once noticed. Flag any of these as trust issues, quoting what the page shows: fake urgency (timers that reset, undated "ends today"), unsourced scarcity ("only 2 left"), confirmshaming decline links, pre-checked marketing or add-on boxes, consent banners where rejecting is harder than accepting, prices that grow with fees later in the flow, and cancellation that is harder than sign-up. Detailed criteria sit in the forms, states and dark patterns checklist.

## Site-Type Specific Guidance

### Personal Brand Sites

Priority order: Messaging clarity > Trust/authority > Content quality > SEO > Conversion
- The person IS the brand — personality must come through
- About page is critical — often the most visited after homepage
- Show don't tell: link to actual work, talks, writing
- One clear primary CTA (usually "book a call" or "hire me")
- Blog/content demonstrates expertise more than claims do

### SaaS / B2B Product Sites

Priority order: Value prop > Conversion flow > Trust signals > SEO > Content
- Homepage must pass the 5-second test
- Pricing transparency reduces friction
- Feature pages need benefit framing
- Integration/ecosystem pages build confidence
- Free trial or demo is expected

### Consulting Sites

Priority order: Credibility > Messaging > Case studies > Conversion > SEO
- Results-oriented language (what clients achieved, not what you do)
- Case studies are the #1 trust builder
- Process transparency ("here's how we work") reduces uncertainty
- Pricing approach: either transparent or clear "book a call" — not ambiguous
- Thought leadership content positions expertise
